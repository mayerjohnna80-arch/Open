The mark

Five ways the O becomes the logo.

Orange and purple, used together — a two-tone identity that's harder to confuse with anyone else. Each direction turns the O of OPEN into the symbol and runs the gradient through it. Shown large, and small on light, so you can see it work as an app icon too.

Color treatment

The palette

Two brand colors, one gradient. Orange brings energy and warmth (the game, the players); purple brings the premium, modern feel that sets it apart from the blue-heavy sports-tech crowd. Together they read as confident and different.

Which mark — and how to choose

  • Aperture O — a camera iris; the gradient blades make it shimmer. Says "film & seeing" most literally, and the two-tone suits it beautifully. Most distinctive.
  • Play O — open ring + play triangle, orange-to-purple sweep. Instantly "video," friendly, safe.
  • Open Ring O — a ring with a gap, gradient along the stroke. "Open" made literal; cleanest at small sizes.
  • Spotlight O — concentric rings from orange core to purple edge; ties to "spotlight the player."
  • Portal O — nested rings opening into depth; most premium and abstract, best if you grow past soccer.

My pick for a two-tone brand: Aperture — the iris blades are made for a gradient and nobody else in this space looks like it — or Open Ring if you want the simplest, most scalable mark.
